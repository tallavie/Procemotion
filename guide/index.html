How to use Procemotion

Six steps from a model to a creature that walks, flies or slithers in your game. Every clip below was recorded from the app with the free sample creatures.

1 · Open2 · Limbs3 · Fix by hand4 · Animate5 · Bake6 · Export

1Open a creature

Drop a GLB, GLTF, FBX or OBJ on the page or use Open model… (bring the texture files along with a GLTF or FBX). Nothing is uploaded: the file stays in your browser. No model at hand? Pick one of the four samples under Try a sample…, or on the empty screen.

  • The model starts walking over a test course of rocks, steps, ramps and stairs at once.
  • A model without bones gets a choice of body plans (two legs, four legs, bird, snake, floating): drag the joints into the body and press Bind skeleton.
  • If the creature faces sideways or backwards, set Faces. If its first pose is odd (lying down, flying), pick a standing clip as the Reference pose.

2Check the limbs it found

Switch to Rig. The skeleton is drawn over the model in colours: green legs, blue arms, purple wings, orange tails, red ears, tan hair and antennae. The Limbs list on the left says what each part is; click a row to light it up, or a joint in the view to see which part it belongs to.

  • A wrong guess? Change the type in the row's menu: the motion follows at once.
  • Ears, tails and tentacles without bones can get some under Add bones: click along the part, or let Find unrigged parts suggest them. Smoother bends adds joints to a stiff tail or snake.

3Fix it by hand when needed

Some models are built in unusual ways. The Bone classes panel on the right of the Rig view lets you say what is what: pick a type, then drag a box over its joints (joints hidden behind the body count too). One box over both legs makes two legs; left and right come by themselves.

  • Select: Single / Multi: one joint per click, or a box.
  • + Add to existing class adds joints you missed to a part that is already there. Shift adds to the selected part, Alt or Remove takes joints out, Ctrl+Z undoes.
  • Head and Pelvis are picked with one click. Every change is saved in your browser for that model; Export JSON takes it to another computer.

The dragon sample was classified this way: its wings are fans of short bones that no rule could read.

4Animate and tune

Back in Animate, the creature moves around the course on its own. Drive lets you steer it with W A S D (Shift to run); In place shows it the way baked clips will look.

  • Movement: speed, stride, step height, how the body rocks and leans. From about half speed a creature with legs gallops; two legs and two arms drop onto all fours.
  • Flight (for wings, or ears used as wings): flap, glide, flap-and-glide or hover; how hard and how fast the wings beat.
  • Springs: how stiff, heavy and wavy each ear, tail or tentacle is. Collision keeps the body and tails out of the ground.
  • Actions play on top of any gait: bite, charge, swipe, stomp, tail whip, roar, hit, faint, jump.

5Bake clips

Under Bake, tick the loops you want (walk, run, idle, slither, flight) and any actions, then press Bake. Each becomes an in-place clip on flat ground that loops seamlessly, listed under Clips where you can play it back. Baked clips work in any engine.

6Export to Unreal Engine

Press Export for Unreal. Pick the Model file (GLB, FBX or both) and set UE import scale so the size line shows the height you want. You get the model with its clips, a .procemotion.json with every setting, and a Python script.

  • In Unreal Engine 5.8, enable the Locomotor (Experimental) and Python Editor Script plugins and import the model.
  • Select the skeletal mesh, then Tools › Execute Python Script and pick the exported script. It builds the Control Rig and BP_name_Procemotion.
  • Drop the actor into a level and press Play: it walks a circle and steps on whatever is below it. BP_name_Procemotion_Run gallops.
  • For your own character: in its Anim Blueprint, put a Control Rig node with the rig after your pose, and move the character with CharacterMovement.

Keep Make the standing pose the skeleton's reference pose on for walking creatures: Unreal's Locomotor starts from that pose.
